# Government and Civil Structure

## Overview

The nation operates on a two-layer system. The Senate is a small oligarchic body that governs at the political level, setting direction, managing national affairs, and holding ultimate authority. Departments are the technical layer, each governing a specific civil or military domain through its own internal structure and expertise. The Senate sets the boundaries; departments operate within them.

This separation exists by design. Political decisions require deliberation across competing priorities. Technical decisions require domain expertise and continuity. Keeping these functions distinct prevents both political interference in operations and technical bodies accumulating unchecked political authority.

## The Senate

The Senate is the highest governing body in the nation. It is a small body of elected members who serve life terms, removable only through impeachment, resignation, or death. Seats are not filled on a regular election cycle; a vacancy opens only when a seat becomes empty, at which point a public election is held to fill it.

The Senate functions as a political oligarchy. Senators are expected to represent distinct political orientations, preventing the body from collapsing into a unified bloc. All major decisions require internal deliberation across those perspectives.

The Senate does not have a standing executive head. A **Senate Chairman** may be designated from among sitting Senators to serve as an organizational lead and public-facing representative for diplomatic meetings, foreign affairs, and formal national communications. The Chairman holds no additional legislative authority. The role is administrative: organizing Senate proceedings, speaking on behalf of a unified Senate position when one exists, and coordinating with department heads. Any Senator may be designated Chairman; the position does not alter their vote or standing within the body.

### Senate Responsibilities

- Declare war and ratify peace
- Negotiate and ratify trade agreements and foreign relations
- Set national priorities and long-term policy direction
- Establish broad constraints for all departments (costs, priorities, land use, funding limits)
- Approve major departmental requests (new infrastructure acquisition, large budget allocations, significant regulatory changes)
- Appoint and dissolve departments
- Resolve cross-department conflicts
- Oversee impeachment proceedings

The Senate does not manage day-to-day operations. It does not write technical regulations or administer services. Its function is to hold authority over what departments may do, not to do those things itself.

## Departments

Departments are the operational layer of the government. Each one governs a specific civil domain and maintains its own internal structure, personnel, and processes suited to its area of work. They handle all execution within the boundaries the Senate sets: policy becomes their problem to implement, technical decisions are theirs to make, and they report back to the Senate when something requires political authority.

Each department is assigned a Senate Liaison, a sitting Senator who maintains the connection between that department and the Senate body. When issues arise that cross department boundaries, the Senate brings the relevant heads together and adjudicates. For a full breakdown of individual departments, see [Departments.md](Departments.md).

## The Military Department

The military operates as a department and reports to the Senate, but holds a categorically different position from civil departments. It is the only department that represents direct physical force at a national scale, which requires a separate accountability structure. For internal organization and rank, see [Military.md](Military.md).

### The Military Committee

The Military Committee sits between the Senate and military command. Its sole purpose is to ensure that military force is directed by national decisions, not individual political ones. No Senator has direct access to military Commanders outside of formal Committee proceedings. For full details, see [Military.md](Military.md).

## Public Participation and Oversight

The public does not vote on legislation or directly direct departments. Public influence operates through:

- Electing Senators when vacancies open
- Public approval and disapproval measures that Senators are politically accountable to
- Formal petitions and class actions directed at departments or the Senate
- Impeachment initiatives against sitting Senators

Public oversight is designed to maintain legitimacy without enabling rapid policy turnover.

## Tribal Territories

Large regions are legally designated as tribal zones, autonomous communities with their own internal governance, culture, and organization. Tribal territories are formally recognized and protected by the Senate, but are not governed by the department system.

Tribes trade with the broader nation and selectively adopt mainstream technology, but do not integrate into departmental civil structure. Some tribes have voluntarily transitioned into standard cities and joined mainstream governance; others have not. The Senate formally recognizes both paths.

## Society at Large

Mainstream civilian life is shaped almost entirely by departments rather than by the Senate directly. Infrastructure, healthcare, energy, transportation, and communications are all departmental functions. Most citizens interact with the government through departments, not through Senate proceedings.

Religion and philosophy are considered private matters. The state does not formally endorse any spiritual interpretation of life, death, or the soul.
