# Government and Civil Structure

## Overview

The nation is governed through a layered deliberative system designed to balance stability, expertise, and public accountability. Authority is centralized at the top but distributed through specialized departments that manage all technical and administrative functions. The system prioritizes slow, deliberate decision-making and continuity over rapid change.

## The Senate

The Senate is the highest governing body of the nation. It is composed of a limited number of elected members who serve life terms unless removed through impeachment, resignation, or death. Seats are filled through public election only when vacancies occur.

Senate composition is structured to maintain ideological diversity. Each seat must represent a distinct political axis, preventing the formation of rigid party blocs and ensuring that multiple perspectives are present in all major decisions.

The Senate does not elect a standing executive leader. Instead, it may designate a temporary representative for diplomatic or ceremonial purposes as needed.

### Senate Responsibilities

- Establish national priorities and long-term direction
- Define legal boundaries and standards for all departments
- Allocate funding between departments
- Resolve conflicts between domains
- Declare war and ratify peace
- Appoint and dissolve departments
- Oversee impeachment proceedings

The Senate does not typically write technical law. Its function is to set constraints, not to manage execution.

## Departments

All operational governance is conducted through departments. Each department governs a specific domain such as infrastructure, energy, health, research, transportation, or defense.

Departments function as semi-autonomous bodies with their own internal leadership, regulatory processes, and administrative structure. Leadership is appointed through a combination of internal selection and Senate approval, depending on the department’s scope and sensitivity.

Each department is assigned a Senate liaison, a sitting Senator responsible for:

- Attending departmental oversight sessions
- Communicating departmental needs to the Senate
- Reporting compliance and conflicts
- Representing departmental interests during budget allocation

Departments may send representatives to testify before the Senate when regulations overlap or conflict with other domains.

Departments may refine or expand regulations within their field, but may not violate Senate-established boundaries.

## Public Participation and Oversight

The public does not vote on legislation directly. Instead, public influence is exercised through:

- Election of Senators when seats open
- Public approval and disapproval measures
- Formal petitions and class actions
- Impeachment initiatives

Public oversight is designed to maintain legitimacy and accountability without enabling rapid shifts in policy.

## Tribal Territories

Large regions of land are designated as tribal zones, inhabited by autonomous communities with distinct cultural, spiritual, and organizational structures. Tribal territories are legally recognized and protected, but operate independently of departmental governance.

Tribes maintain their own internal systems of organization and collective decision-making. While they adopt certain technologies and trade with the broader nation, they are not fully integrated into the departmental system.

Some tribes have voluntarily transitioned into standard cities and joined mainstream governance. Others maintain separation. The Senate formally recognizes both paths as legitimate.

## Society at Large

Mainstream society is highly organized, technologically advanced, and dependent on departmental coordination. Daily life is shaped primarily by the actions of departments rather than direct Senate involvement.

Public trust in the system is maintained through:

- transparency of process
- predictable governance
- long-term stability
- visible infrastructure and services

Religion and philosophy vary widely, but are generally considered private matters. No single spiritual interpretation of life or death is formally endorsed by the state.
