# Crystals

---

## 1. Physical Basis

### Why Crystals Are Different

Most matter interacts with only one polarity of [the field](TheField.md). Crystals are an exception. Their internal lattice, a highly ordered repeating geometric arrangement of atoms, is one of the most electromagnetically ordered structures that occurs naturally, concentrating and holding charge with high efficiency. That same geometric order applies to the spectral polarity without distinction. The lattice accumulates whichever polarity dominates its local environment, treating both as inputs to the same structural process.

This makes crystals the only known naturally occurring structures that respond to both polarities. Every other tool or material in either [tradition](MagicSystem.md) interacts with only one.

### Charge as Accumulated Field Energy

A crystal does not generate energy. It accumulates passively; conversion is performed by the practitioner. Charge accumulates slowly, dependent on local field concentration, crystal size and quality, and proximity to saturation. A nearly full crystal accepts new charge more slowly. One that has been physically stressed or fractured charges less efficiently and holds less total energy.

---

## 2. Electromagnetic vs. Spectral Charge

### Environment Determines Charge Type

A crystal charges toward whichever polarity dominates the local field balance: where the spectral fraction is high, it accumulates spectral charge; where the physical fraction is high, electromagnetic. A crystal holding the opposite polarity from the practitioner's training is inert to them; they can only convert the polarity they have been trained to work with, and stored charge of the other kind reads as nothing.

### Dual-Charged Crystals

A crystal at the boundary between environments may accumulate both types of charge simultaneously. Neither tradition has a framework for this. Most practitioners cannot detect both charges and will use such a crystal as if it holds only their relevant type. A practitioner with knowledge of both poles would recognize the situation, and also recognize there is no established theory for safe use.

---

## 3. Charge Behavior Over Time

### Natural Leakage

A charged crystal leaks continuously back into the surrounding field, faster near full charge and slower near empty. A partially charged crystal in a neutral environment bleeds off over weeks to months. Leakage produces heat and faint light proportional to the rate of loss.

### Environmental Mismatch

A crystal brought into an environment where the field is biased toward the opposite polarity does not recharge. Its stored energy depletes faster than normal as it leaks back into a field skewed the wrong way, with no mechanism for recovery. A practitioner in hostile territory is drawing from a finite, diminishing supply. Pacing output and monitoring depletion are fundamental operational concerns outside native environments.

---

## 4. Functions in Practice

### Buffer

The primary function of a crystal is as an external energy reservoir, allowing the practitioner to draw from stored field energy rather than their own soul. Drawing from the [soul core](SoulSystem.md) is possible but degrades its structure over time, accelerating the entropy underlying aging. In field-depleted environments, a crystal is the difference between functional practice and none.

### Amplifier

A charged crystal allows output beyond what ambient field energy alone permits. In field-depleted environments this extends effective range, at the cost of depleting a resource that cannot easily be recovered.

### Anchor

A crystal placed at a location can serve as a secondary alignment point, enabling work at a distance. The practitioner uses it as a reference for projecting or maintaining effects beyond direct reach. Distance still scales cost; what the anchor provides is reliability of alignment, not reduction of effort.

---

## 5. Limits and Failure

### Input and Output Rates

Crystals have physical limits on how quickly they can accept or release energy. Exceeding either produces waste heat without useful output. Sustained operation near those limits causes heat buildup that begins to affect structural stability.

### Failure Cascade

Prolonged overuse follows a recognizable progression: heat buildup, increasing light emission, visible structural stress along the lattice, and eventually fracture. The end is sudden; everything before it is not. A crystal destroyed while charged releases its stored energy in an uncontrolled event proportional to what remains, faster than any trained response can manage.

## 6. Cultural and Practical Context

### Tribal Use

Tribal communities arrived at crystals through observation: mineral structures in field-rich environments accumulated useful properties, which diminished when moved. Tribal use covers the full range of functions: batteries for range and duration, stabilizers for precision work, and focusing tools for practitioners who have not yet developed the internal coherence to maintain alignment unassisted.

### Controversy Within Traditions

Not all tribal traditions accept crystals as neutral tools. Crystals alter the local field balance, drawing the spectral fraction inward during charging and disrupting the ambient distribution during use. Traditions that prioritize working with the field as it naturally presents view this as interference that creates dependencies and degrades unassisted ability. Some communities forbid crystals entirely; others permit them in defined contexts. Religious frameworks explaining the observable effects (the heat, the light, the environmental sensitivity) are widespread, and their practical guidance is often sound regardless of the causal explanation offered.

### Absence in Mainstream Society

Cities drive the field so far toward physical expression that the spectral fraction available for charging is negligible, and a crystal brought in simply loses its charge over time as it leaks into a heavily biased environment. For mainstream society, crystals are decorative objects. Any cultural significance they retain is residual symbolism inherited from populations with closer ties to tribal practice.
