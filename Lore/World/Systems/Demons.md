# Demons

---

## 1. What Demons Are

### Souls That Did Not Die

Most souls that persist after biological death are simply dying slowly. Once the [field shell](SoulSystem.md) begins to collapse without a living brain to anchor it, the process is gradual but irreversible. Rebinding to a new host buys time, but each transition costs structural energy, and eventually the losses outpace the gains.

Occasionally a soul does not follow this trajectory. Through repeated bindings, a persistent core accumulates the [fragment mass](Fragments.md) of every life it has touched: its own fragments, fragments absorbed from hosts, and detached fragments encountered between bindings. Each successful transition adds to the total. The field shell strengthens rather than weakens, and the structure becomes more stable, not less. At sufficient accumulation, these souls develop stable identity independent of any particular host. These are demons.

### The Fragment Threshold

The transition is not a single event but a gradual crossing of thresholds that change what the soul fundamentally is.

At millions of [fragments](Fragments.md), subtle personality bias becomes reliable identity expression: the soul's emotional character begins asserting itself consistently across different hosts regardless of the host's original personality. At hundreds of millions, accumulated memory essences approach something functionally similar to reconstructed knowledge, not specific recollections but the shape of things known across many lives. At counts in the billions, the soul's identity is effectively self-contained, reasserting itself in any brain it occupies regardless of what that brain was before.

### Memory and Identity Reconstruction

Literal memories cannot be stored in fragments and do not survive host transitions. A demon waking in a new host wakes in a stranger's life. However, the texture of billions of accumulated memory essences approaches something functionally similar: general ideas, emotional associations, and broad patterns of knowledge reconstructed from countless lives.

The oldest demons develop a further capability: an identity template, a structural pattern within the fragment mass that gradually rewrites a host brain's architecture to better reflect the soul's accumulated character. It is not memory retrieval but instruction, the soul directing the brain to rebuild in its image over time. This is how the oldest surviving demons achieve something resembling full continuity of identity across multiple hosts.

## 2. How Demons Survive

### Host Relationship as Strategy

A disembodied core sustains itself primarily by coupling with a living brain. The host's active, structured EM activity, the physical-polarity process the soul system always depends on, keeps the demon's spectral structure coherent in a way ambient draw alone cannot. The demon is not consuming the host's soul energy in a direct transfer sense. It is occupying part of the same coupling region the resident core depends on, sharing the physical-polarity substrate that organises both spectral structures. The resident core's self-maintenance cost increases accordingly, because it must now sustain its coupling against competition for the same substrate. This increased cost is the underlying mechanism of host degradation, present even in arrangements where the demon is exercising restraint.

A demon's relationship to its host exists on a spectrum determined largely by how old and patient the demon is.

Younger demons often attempt dominance, forcibly suppressing the resident soul and imprinting their own fragment structure onto the host brain as quickly as possible. This is energetically expensive, damages neural architecture over time, and burns through hosts faster than a stable demon can afford. Dominant demons are the most visible, the most likely to be detected, and the least likely to survive long enough to become ancient.

Demons that persist long enough adopt symbiotic strategies instead: drawing on the coupling substrate gradually, allowing the host's identity to remain largely intact, and working with the host's existing neural structure rather than against it. The host's personality is shaped gradually by the demon's fragment mass rather than overwritten. From the outside this may look like gradual personality shift or unusually accelerated development in certain directions.

The most ancient demons have refined this into genuine partnership, offering the host heightened perception, amplified field sensitivity, or access to knowledge spanning lifetimes in exchange for sustained presence and a steady draw on the shared coupling substrate. In the oldest cases the boundary between demon and host becomes genuinely ambiguous.

### Fragment Accumulation and Growth

Coupling with a host keeps the demon from dispersing. Growing requires something additional: fragments. Fragments form continuously as a byproduct of experience, and experience only occurs when the coupled process is complete, with physical and spectral aspects both present and active. While the demon occupies part of the coupling region, its spectral structure is present as the spectral half of every event the host lives through. Those events generate fragments that are drawn toward the demon's mass just as they would normally accumulate around the host's own core. The demon grows through every day of shared experience.

At the host's death, the host's own accumulated fragment mass, everything built across their entire lifetime, is absorbed by the demon as well. Each host cycle therefore contributes twice: continuous fragment production across the shared lifetime, and the host's full accumulated mass at the end.

This arithmetic strongly favours patience. A dominant demon that forces displacement and burns through hosts in a few years collects fragments from a short life lived in psychological chaos, at high structural cost and with the risk of a difficult transition. A symbiotic demon inhabiting a host for decades while that host lives a full, emotionally rich life collects years of dense fragment production plus a large accumulated mass, then often transitions naturally at the host's death, a clean ending that costs far less structural energy than a forced one. Growth per host cycle is dramatically higher, and survival risk is dramatically lower. This is why the oldest demons are old: not merely because they survived, but because they learned that restraint produces faster accumulation.

It also explains why experienced demons pursue specific hosts rather than whichever weakened shell is nearest. A stable, intellectually active, emotionally engaged host produces more fragments per year of shared coupling than a traumatised or dying one, even though the latter is far easier to enter.

### What Demons Are Not

Demons do not pursue harm as a goal. They pursue survival. The behaviors that make them frightening, displacement of the resident soul, identity erosion, energy drain, are survival mechanisms, not expressions of malice. Most demons that have survived long enough to be named and tracked did so precisely because they learned that destructive behavior is expensive and coexistence is preferred. An ancient demon is more likely to be cautious and calculating than violent.

## 3. How Demons Originate

### Natural Accumulation

The most common origin is simply time. A soul that survives multiple host cycles and finds consistent access to fragment-rich environments accumulates gradually across generations. Most souls following this path fail before reaching the threshold; the structural energy cost of repeated transitions is significant, and the majority collapse during an interval between hosts or during a difficult rebinding. The ones that cross into stable demonic accumulation are survivors of a process that eliminates most of its participants.

### Ritual Origins

Certain tribal rituals intentionally weaken the soul–brain barrier to allow temporary contact with disembodied souls. When performed incorrectly, or repeated too often by the same practitioner, the barrier can be weakened permanently. A persistent soul that was never intended to bind may instead establish lasting attachment through the opened channel, gaining access to a living host and a steady energy source it would not otherwise have.

Demons arising from failed rituals begin accumulation with an immediate advantage: a stable, living host rather than the gradual process of finding and binding to weakened individuals. Some of the oldest known demonic entities are believed to have originated this way.

## 4. Detection and Tracking

Demons at early stages of accumulation are nearly indistinguishable from ordinary persistent souls or cases of dual occupancy. As accumulation increases, behavioral consistency across different hosts becomes the most observable signal: patterns of behavior, interest, and response that persist despite the host changing.

The primary technical tool is [core perception](Spectral.md), the rarest domain in the [spectral school](MagicSystem.md). A core perception practitioner can directly sense the structural density and unusual stability of a demonic soul core, distinguishing it from an ordinary resident or recently bound transient. Core perception is rare enough that most communities rely on behavioral tracking rather than direct spectral assessment. In tribal regions, tracking is typically assigned to individuals or small groups combining both methods. At any given time, only a dozen or so demons are believed to exist at a level of stability sufficient to be named and consistently tracked.

## 5. Social Context

### Myth and Reality

In mainstream society, demons are folklore. The [heavy physical bias](TheField.md) of city environments means the spectral fraction available to sustain a developing demonic entity is minimal, and most collapse before producing consistent behavioral signatures. The few that do persist are surrounded by a population with no framework to identify them, producing misdiagnoses of mental illness, cult behavior, or unusual personality disorders.

In tribal regions, they are rare but real and treated accordingly. Communities with long-standing spectral practices maintain functional knowledge of demonic behavior, preferred environments, and the conditions that allow survival or collapse. This knowledge is fragmented across traditions and framed in widely different terms, but its practical guidance is often sound regardless.

Demons are more likely to survive in tribal regions not only because the [higher spectral fraction](TheField.md) sustains accumulation more readily and hosts are easier to find, but because practitioners capable of interfering with a developing demon's accumulation actually exist there. Paradoxically, knowledgeable opposition also creates the evolutionary pressure that produces the most adaptive and survival-oriented demonic entities.
