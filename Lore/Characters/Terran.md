# Terran Addari

**Species:** King Cheetah\
**Gender:** Male\
**Age:** 25\
**Height:** 180 cm\
**Weight:** 85 kg\
**Rank:** Lieutenant\
**Specialist:** Spec-ops

## Overview

Raised with the high expectations of his father looming over him, Terran has been trained to climb the ranks of the military his whole childhood. He was always expected to be perfect, and his accomplishments reflected perfection. Though by the time he was 18 he appeared to be a highly skilled soldier, his heart secretly yearned for a more fulfilling path that he could choose for himself. In his spare time, He enjoys hobbies such as reading and writing short stories. He also keeps a detailed journal of his daily events and happenings, sheerly out of habit. He treats his squad with clinical indifference, and maintains clear order and efficiency. He excells at handling stressful situations, remaining calm and composed to the point that some see him as emotionless. As this is the case, many of his subordinates find him unapproachable. Through his own skill, as well as his father's meddling, he has soared through the ranks at a pace faster than any other soldier before him. He is only 25 when he is initially being considdered for a promotion to captain, an unprecidented rank for his age. While he appears well off on the surface, his need for his father's approval and lack of personal fulfillment seethe underneath his facade.

Just before his promotion is finalized, Terran is tasked with arresting and interrogating a civilian academic from the university, a student whose paper on historical political patterns has apparently attracted military attention. He is presented with briefings claiming the individual is a dangerous radical connected to a terrorist organization plotting against the military. However, the classified intelligence suggests the true issue is the student's analysis of a recent opposition protest, in which Terran begins to suspect the military may have deliberately mischaracterized the group's actual message. Nevertheless, his orders are clear: the student represents a security threat and must be brought in for questioning. The assignment troubles him more than he would admit, not least because silencing a college paper feels inconsistent with the military's stated purpose. His father's influence and his trajectory toward captain suggest he should not question his orders.
