# Rathick "Rath"

**Species:** Cheetah\
**Gender:** Male\
**Age:** 26\
**Height:** 180 cm\
**Weight:** 85 kg\
**Rank:** Sergeant\
**Specialist:** Elite Combat\
**Element:** Thermal

## Overview

Rathick, called Rath by everyone, grew up in the same tribal community as Kit as one of its better thermal manipulators. He was physically capable, socially at ease, and skilled enough to have real standing in a community that measured worth through elemental ability and physical discipline. He wasn't loud about any of it. He was well-liked because he was competent and consistent, not because he sought attention. In his downtime he was quieter than people expected, more interested in sitting in comfortable silence or talking about things most of his peers didn't find worth discussing. Kit was the only person who matched that. They became close the way people do when they realize they're the only two in a room actually saying anything.

When the tribe began its gradual collapse, its members drifting toward nearby mainstream society one by one for the stability and comfort city life offered, Rath stayed. It wasn't a difficult decision. He respected Kit more than most of the people who were leaving, and he could see clearly that Kit was going to be on his own if someone didn't make a different choice. Half of his reason was genuine regard. The other half was something closer to protectiveness that he wouldn't have described that way out loud. He stayed, and they figured out city life together.

They settled in the adjacent town, learned the common language properly, found work, and got by. Their elemental abilities functioned as novelties. The town knew about tribal casting well enough to find it interesting, but had no practical framework for it. Rath adapted to city life more outwardly than Kit did, though neither of them fully belonged. On an ordinary day at a market, Rath saw a pickpocket and intervened without much calculation, acting on the kind of instinct he had always been prone to. A military recruiter witnessed the intervention and recognized the thermal output as combat-applicable. The offer came to Rath alone. He agreed to enlist on one condition: Kit came with him.

At intake training, Rath was immediately a standout. His physical conditioning, elemental ability, and composure under pressure moved him fast. He was selected for the elite training track within the first phase, the kind of result that draws attention and shapes a career. Before he fully committed, he negotiated with his handlers to let Kit find a placement in a different capacity. The negotiation was non-negotiable from his end. He didn't frame it as a favor. He framed it as a condition. Once Kit found his footing in avionics, Rath continued.

In the elite program, Rath earned a reputation as someone who is hard to read under pressure and harder to unsettle. He does not perform calm. He is calm, trained in an environment that treated composure as a survival condition rather than a social skill. He follows orders when they make sense to him and his own sense of responsibility, and he doesn't pretend otherwise. His superiors have mostly found this tolerable because his results are clean and his judgment, in practice, tends to be sound. The ones who have found it less tolerable have generally learned to work around it. He earns trust through what he does rather than what he says, and holds only a small, carefully chosen circle close.

Through the elite track, Rath is exposed to the military's inner structure at a level most sergeants never see, including its politics, its key figures, and the institutional machinery that people like Terran operate inside. He is not political by nature. What he is, is observant. He notices patterns the way a person does when they've learned to read situations fast and without illusions. The patterns he begins noticing during act 1 are not ones that sit comfortably alongside the code he operates by.

His most complicated relationship heading into act 1 is with Kit. What they have built together, the shared language, the single question that stops Rath before he makes the wrong call, the humor nobody else catches, does not prepare him for who Kit is after the binding. The reserve is the same but the insecurity is gone, replaced by something more settled and more forward. Rath recognizes the change as real and is not sure of his place in relation to it. He is protective of Kit in a way that is now partly reversed, and he hasn't worked out how to hold that yet.

## Elemental Ability

Rath is a thermal manipulator, one of the stronger ones his tribe produced. He generates and redirects heat, with combat applications that range from sustained heat pressure to precise thermal bursts. His control is practical rather than showy; he learned it through physical repetition alongside his broader conditioning, so it functions as an extension of his body rather than a separate skill set. The military recognized its combat value immediately on recruitment. In the field it is treated as a force multiplier alongside his physical ability, not a replacement for it.

## Origins

Grew up in the same tribal community as Kit. He practiced thermal manipulation, a discipline that was well regarded in his community. He learned it through physical repetition rather than formal instruction, giving him strong situational awareness and bodily judgment that carries directly into everything he does in the military.

## Relationships

**Kit:** The oldest and most important relationship he has. He stayed when everyone else left, and that choice defines the shape of everything that followed. He is the only person who reliably catches Kit's humor. After Kit's binding, he is also the one most unsettled by the change, and the one whose reaction Kit actually cares about.
