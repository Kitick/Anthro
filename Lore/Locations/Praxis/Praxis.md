# Nation of Praxis

## Overview

The Senate is a oligarchic body that governs at the political level: setting direction, managing national affairs, and holding ultimate authority over Praxis. The senate delegates authority by creating administrative departments, that each govern a specific civil or military domain through their own internal structure and expertise.

This separation of powers exists by design. Political decisions require an audience with the senate, wherin individual departments that are deemed relevant to the issue being decided can send delegates. Keeping these functions distinct prevents both political interference in operations and technical bodies accumulating unchecked political authority.


## The Senate

A small body of elected members who serve life terms, removable only through impeachment, resignation, or death. Seats are not filled on a regular election cycle; a vacancy opens only when a seat becomes empty, at which point a public election is held to fill it.

The Senate functions as a political oligarchy. Senators are expected to represent distinct political orientations, preventing the body from collapsing into a unified bloc. Potential senators are vetted thorugh a personality test and interviews from the existing active senators. All major decisions require internal deliberation across current senate members.

The Senate does not have a standing executive head. A **Senate Chairman** may be designated from among sitting Senators to serve as an organizational lead and public-facing representative for diplomatic meetings, foreign affairs, and formal national communications. The Chairman holds no additional legislative authority. The role is administrative: organizing Senate proceedings, speaking on behalf of a unified Senate position when one exists, and coordinating with department heads. Any Senator may be designated Chairman, the position does not alter their vote or standing within the body, but they recieve additional compensation while holding the title.


### Senate Responsibilities

- Declaring war and ratifying peace
- Negotiating and ratifying trade agreements and foreign relations
- Setting national priorities and long-term policy directions
- Establishing broad constraints for all departments (costs, priorities, land use, funding limits)
- Approving major departmental requests (new infrastructure acquisition, large budget allocations, significant regulatory changes)
- Creating and dissolving departments
- Resolving cross-departmental conflicts
- Oversees impeachment proceedings

The Senate does not manage day-to-day operations. It does not write technical regulations or administer services. Its function is to hold authority over what departments can and cannot do, not to take regulatory action.

### Public Participation and Oversight

The public does not vote on legislation or directly influence departments. Public influence operates through:

- Electing Senators when vacancies open
- Senators accrue a public approval measure. When disapproval reaches a certain level they are eligable for impeachment
- Formal petitions and class actions directed at departments or the Senate
- Impeachment initiatives against sitting Senators


## [Departments](Departments.md)

Departments are the regulatory body of the government. Each one governs a specific civil domain and maintains its own internal structure, personnel, and processes suited to its area of work. They handle all execution within the boundaries the Senate sets: policy becomes their problem to implement, technical decisions are theirs to make, and they report back to the Senate when something requires political authority.

Each department is assigned a Senate delegate, a figure who has the authority to respresent and vote on the behalf of their department.


## [The Military](Military.md)

The military operates as a department and reports to the Senate, but holds a categorically different position from civil departments. It is the only department that represents direct physical force at a national scale, which requires a separate accountability structure.


### The Military Committee

The Military Committee sits between the Senate and military command. Its sole purpose is to ensure that military force is directed by national decisions, not individual political ones. No Senator has direct access to military Flag Officers outside of formal Committee proceedings.
